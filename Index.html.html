1. Executive summary

Commissioning and testing of a data center's electrical systems (utility feeds, switchgear, transformers, generators, ATS, UPS, PDUs) produces large volumes of test evidence, approvals and asset data. This information is typically scattered across spreadsheets, email threads and vendor reports, which makes it hard to prove readiness, trace issues or hand over cleanly to operations.

This initiative proposes using ServiceNow as the system of record for electrical assets, commissioning activities, change approvals, test evidence and ongoing preventive maintenance. Where possible it uses out-of-box capabilities (CMDB, Change, Asset, Incident, Event Management) and adds a small custom scoped application for the commissioning workflow and structured test data.

Expected outcome: one traceable record per critical electrical asset, from factory test to commissioning to day-2 operations, with approvals, evidence and impact analysis available in one place.

2. Business problem

  • Fragmented records: test results, checklists and sign-offs live in different files and tools, with no single source of truth.
  • Weak traceability: it is difficult to show which test was performed on which asset, by whom, and with what result.
  • Risky energization and shutdown activities: method of procedure (MOP) approvals and live-work permits are managed informally, with limited impact analysis.
  • Handover gaps: operations inherit incomplete asset data and no maintenance baseline.
  • Reactive maintenance: preventive tasks (breaker testing, battery checks, thermography, generator runs) are tracked inconsistently.
  • Audit burden: evidence for tier certification, insurance and regulatory reviews takes significant effort to assemble.

4. Scope

In scope

  • Electrical asset registration and relationship mapping (utility to rack PDU).
  • Commissioning workflow across Levels 1 to 5 (factory test, delivery, installation checks, functional tests, integrated systems test).
  • Structured capture of test results, deficiencies (punch list) and sign-offs.
  • Change and MOP approval for energization, shutdown and live work.
  • Preventive maintenance scheduling and work orders.
  • Alarm and event intake from monitoring systems, with incident creation.
  • Reporting, dashboards and an evidence pack for handover and audit.

Out of scope

  • Replacing power system analysis tools (short-circuit, coordination, arc-flash studies).
  • Replacing the building management or electrical power monitoring systems.
  • Mechanical and cooling commissioning (possible later phase).
  • Procurement and contractor invoicing.

6. Current state

Typical conditions in the absence of a managed solution:

  • Equipment lists maintained in spreadsheets, often out of date by handover.
  • Test scripts and results exchanged by email as PDF or Excel files.
  • MOP and permit approvals tracked in separate documents or tools.
  • No linkage between electrical assets and the IT services that depend on them.
  • Maintenance tracked by calendar reminders or a separate facilities tool.

9. Non-functional requirements

  • Security: role-based access (ACLs), separation of duties between tester and approver, and an audit trail on all records.
  • Auditability: test results are retained and cannot be altered after sign-off without a logged exception.
  • Usability: technicians can record a test result in a few minutes, including from a tablet or phone.
  • Maintainability: a scoped application, minimal customization of base tables, and upgrade-safe configuration.
  • Performance: forms and reports remain responsive with thousands of assets and tens of thousands of test records.
  • Compliance alignment: supports evidence needs for Uptime Institute tiering, CSA Z462 safety practice and the Canadian Electrical Code.

13. Reporting and KPIs

  • Commissioning progress by level, system and site.
  • Open deficiencies by severity, owner and age.
  • Test pass rate and first-time pass rate by asset type.
  • Change approval cycle time for high-risk work.
  • Preventive maintenance compliance and overdue tasks.
  • Power-related incidents by asset and root cause.

14. Risks and assumptions

Assumptions

  • A ServiceNow instance with CMDB, Change and Incident is available; additional plugins to be confirmed.
  • Equipment data and test scripts can be supplied by contractors in a structured format.
  • A CMDB owner exists and agrees to the class and naming approach.

16. Acceptance criteria

  1. All critical electrical assets in scope exist as CIs with required fields and relationships.
  2. For a pilot system, every required test has a recorded result, tester, date and attachment.
  3. A level cannot be signed off while critical deficiencies remain open.
  4. An energization or shutdown cannot proceed without an approved change and MOP.
  5. An evidence pack for the pilot system can be generated without manual assembly.
  6. Role-based access and the audit trail are verified in testing.
